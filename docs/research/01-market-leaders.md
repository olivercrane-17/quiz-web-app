# 01. Market leaders: quiz and trivia case studies

Researched 2026-10-31 with about 75 web searches, preferring primary sources. Duolingo's blog and Lenny's Newsletter could not be fetched directly; their figures come from search excerpts. Tags: **[F]** filing or company-reported, **[P]** press or academic, **[S]** secondary (low confidence), and *Our read* for interpretation rather than evidence.

## Summary

- **Entry takes seconds.** A PIN (Kahoot), a link (Wordle), a friend's pending turn (Trivia Crack) or a phone on your forehead (Heads Up!). Kahoot reports 10B+ cumulative participants [F], and NYT players solved 11.2B puzzles in 2025 [P].
- **A shared object starts the talk.** The same daily puzzle for everyone, or the same question on one screen. Wordle averaged 46 shared scores per second in 2025 [P].
- **Breakouts grew through built-in sharing, not ad spend.** Wordle's spoiler-free grid, HQ's referral lives, QuizUp barely touching its $1M acquisition fund and Trivia Crack's unpaid chart run [P].
- **Habit comes from capped sessions and forgiving streaks.** Duolingo has 10M+ year-long streaks and a third of DAUs on a Friend Streak [F]. LinkedIn says 84% of its puzzle players return the next day [F].
- **Group play needs a way back for weaker players.** Blooket's chests and Gimkit's economy stop the same few kids from always winning [F/S].
- **Questions are the moat and the liability.** By one estimate, Trivia Crack published roughly 1 in 200 user submissions [P]. One editor has written every Connections board [P].
- **The famous failures were economic.** HQ paid out about $1M a month in prizes [S], and QuizUp's maker blamed community costs [P].
- **AI quiz generation is table stakes** (Kahoot, Wayground, NotebookLM, Gizmo). What stays scarce is game feel and trust in the answers.
- **Money comes from the edges.** Business plans subsidize Kahoot's free teacher tier, Sporcle is about 90% ads, NYT bundles Games into subscriptions and Duolingo has 12.7M subscribers [F/P].

## Detailed findings

### Hosted play in the same room

**Kahoot! (2013).** *Loop:* a question appears on a shared screen and players answer on phones after typing a PIN. Correct answers earn up to 1,000 points weighted by speed, plus a streak bonus; an Accuracy mode ignores speed ([Kahoot help](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/articles/115002303908)) [F]. *Origin:* NTNU professor Alf Inge Wang dreamed of TV-style game shows played in the same room ([Innomag](https://www.innomag.no/?p=17683), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/11/02/kahoot-how-a-student-professor-duo-launched-a-5point7-billion-tech-idea.html)). *Growth:* "To play Kahoot! is to promote Kahoot!" says the 2021 investor deck ([deck](https://kahoot.com/files/2021/06/Kahoot_Company_Presentation_2021_06_03.pdf)), and schools are where it "first went viral" ([blog](https://kahoot.com/blog/2023/11/09/kahoot-reaches-10-billion/)) [F]. *Retention:* five months of every-lecture use produced only a slight engagement drop ([Wang 2015](https://folk.idi.ntnu.no/alfw/publications/Wearout_effect_of_game_based_student_response_system.pdf)) [P]. *Money and audience:* free for teachers, with business customers subsidizing schools ([EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2018-09-21-kahoot-launches-first-ever-premium-version-for-schools)). ARR hit $149M in Q3 2022 ([Euronext](https://live.euronext.com/en/products/equities/company-news/2022-11-03-kahoot-group-reports-third-quarter-2022-financial-results)) [F], and a 2023 take-private valued it near $1.7B ([multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/kahoot)) [S]. *Metrics:* 10B+ non-unique participants by November 2023, and 1.6B in the trailing year per the 2024 press kit ([kit](https://kahoot.com/files/2017/08/Kahoot-PressKit-2024.pdf)) [F]. *Weak spot:* open PINs invite bot floods and rude names, hence a friendly-name generator ([support forum](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360029558133-Kahoot-Hacks-must-stop)) [F].

**Quizlet Live (2016).** Prototyped in a day at a hackathon ([Shipping on Fridays](https://shippingonfridays.substack.com/p/hitting-the-hackathon-jackpot-how)) and launched in April 2016 ([EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2016-04-12-quizlet-s-first-product-for-the-classroom-is-live)). Students land on random teams of three or four. Only one teammate's screen holds the right answer, so teams must talk. Accuracy beats speed ([JALT](https://jalt-publications.org/sites/default/files/pdf-article/40.6tlt-wired.pdf)) [P]. *Our read:* random teams end playground picking. An insider says it reached about 10% of Quizlet traffic within a year [S].

**Gimkit (2017) and Blooket (public around 2020).** Both bolt a game economy onto multiple choice. Gimkit began as a high schooler's project ([KIRO 7](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/issaquah-student-launches-online-education-business-from-high-school/955941411/)). Correct answers pay virtual cash for upgrades and power-ups, some of which hinder rivals, and teachers can disable those ([Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/gimkit)) [P]. In Blooket's Gold Quest, each correct answer opens one of three chests: gold, a multiplier, nothing, a steal, a swap or a gold-eating dragon ([Blooket help](https://help.blooket.com/hc/en-us/articles/42100784373655-Game-Overview-Gold-Quest)) [F]. *Our read:* chance and strategy mean the fastest reader does not always win, so everyone keeps playing. Both are freemium and reportedly bootstrapped ([revenuememo](https://www.revenuememo.com/p/who-owns-blooket)) [S], and they spread teacher to teacher. *Weak spot:* game state in the browser. One "Blooket Hacks" userscript lists 12,000+ installs ([Greasy Fork](https://greasyfork.org/ru/scripts/463382-blooket-hacks/code)), and Gimkit cheats abound ([Greasy Fork](https://greasyfork.org/scripts/by-site/gimkit.com)) [P].

### Duels

**Trivia Crack / Preguntados (2013).** *Loop:* spin a six-category wheel and answer; three correct answers earn a crown to claim a category character. Collect all six within 25 rounds, or duel to steal one ([GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/reviews/review-trivia-crack-will-challenge-your-knowledge-and-friendships/)) [P]. Turns take a minute or two, while matches run for days. *Growth and audience:* async play with family and friends, born in Argentina. The founder told the BBC it hit the top charts without paid ads ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-37630981)). It was the world's top download in early 2015 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/trivia-crack-is-the-top-downloaded-app-in-the-world/)) and passed 500M downloads by 2018 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/news/69128/trivia-crack-sequel-launch-original-passes-500m-downloads/)) [P]. *Content:* the Question Factory lets players submit, rate, translate and flag questions; by one estimate about 1,000 of every 200,000 submissions reach the game ([Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/trivia-crack-hit-growing-pains-thanks-flood-user-submitted-questions)) [P]. Question sets are written per region rather than simply translated ([Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/marketing-strategies/app-and-mobile/trivia-crack-wins-globally-by-staying-local)). *Money:* ads via AdMob ([AdMob](https://admob.google.com/home/resources/admob-helps-trivia-crack-go-global-and-reach-over-millions-of-users/)) plus lives, spins, coins and gems, a mix one analyst called "conspicuously aggressive" next to QuizUp ([Mobile Dev Memo](https://mobiledevmemo.com/trivia-crack-case-study-virality)). It still made about $40K a week on US iOS in Q2 2024 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q2-ios-top-5-trivia%20games-revenue-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)) [P].

**QuizUp (2013 to 2016).** *Loop:* real-time 1v1 on a chosen topic: seven questions, 10 seconds each, up to 20 points for speed, last question doubled ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QuizUp)) [P]. *Growth:* 1M users on day eight ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/this-is-the-fastest-growing-iphone-game-ever/articleshow/26918487.cms)) and 10M in four months ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2014/03/06/how-hit-trivia-app-quizup-survived-the-hype-cycle/)), with 400+ topics by March 2014 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/20/quizup-1m-android)). The CEO spent only a fraction of a $1M acquisition fund ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/we-had-a-1-million-ua-launch-fund-but-only-spent-a-fraction-says-plain-vanilla-ceo-of-us-1-hit-quizup/)) [P]. *End:* despite a $22M Sequoia-led round ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/26/quizup-22m-sequoia-botha/)), the company said in 2016 that community costs outran revenue. NBC dropped a planned TV show and all 36 staff were let go ([Iceland Monitor](https://icelandmonitor.mbl.is/news/news/2016/08/31/quizup_creators_plain_vanilla_close_iceland_office), [Grapevine](https://grapevine.is/?p=9952610)) [P].

**Jeopardy! World Tour (2017).** Uken and Sony's free-to-play take on the show ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=139653)), with async matches against real people ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/jeopardy-world-tour)) and an Apple Arcade edition ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6444295014)). It made roughly $24K to $33K a week in US revenue in Q3 2024 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q3-unified-top-5-trivia%20games-revenue-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)) [P]. *Our read:* a known format skips the tutorial.

### Appointment live shows

**HQ Trivia (2017 to 2020).** *Loop:* a live host at 3pm and 9pm ET read 12 three-option questions with about 10 seconds each. One miss and you were out; survivors split the pot ([TIME](https://time.com/5038037/hq-trivia-app-game/)) [P]. *Growth:* a referral code gave both sides an extra life ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/27/heres-an-easy-trick-to-get-an-extra-life-on-hq-trivia-without-a-referral/)). It went from 3,300 players in October 2017 to about 2.38M concurrent by March 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/06/hq-trivia-questions/)) and raised $15M at a $100M valuation ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2018/03/06/hq-trivia-15-million-100-million-valuation/)) [P]. Sponsors paid for special games, including a reported $3M Warner Bros. deal ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/hq-trivia-cashes-in-with-new-social-features-dwayne-johnson-appearance/521093/)). *Decline:* lag and a $20 cash-out minimum ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1370704/hq-trivia-most-disastrous-moments/)), slow payouts ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/hq-trivia-player-says-he-won-20-000-weeks-ago-but-still-hasnt-been-paid.255169/)), prizes near $1M a month ([Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/story/what-happened-to-hq-trivia-business-model)) [S], installs down 92% ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/HQ-Trivia-and-Words-installs-drop-92-as-it-plans-to-charge-a-monthly-subscription-fee_id117281)) and a product that "ceased to evolve" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/14/hq-trivia-shuts-down)) [P]. It shut down in February 2020.

### Evergreen quizzes and casual play

**Sporcle (2007).** A sports site that pivoted after a US presidents quiz took off ([Sporcle blog](https://www.sporcle.com/blog/2017/01/what-is-sporcle-a-brief-history)). *Loop:* timed quizzes where typed answers register the instant they match ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3129298/this-addictive-quiz-site-is-trivia-night-on-demand.html)), lasting 30 seconds to 20 minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporcle)). *Growth:* user-made quizzes since 2009, with editors featuring the best each day ([interview](https://www.sramanamitra.com/?p=143057)) [P]. *Our read:* thousands of evergreen quiz pages double as search traffic. *Money:* "probably 90% ads," with an ad-free tier on the side (same interview). *Metrics:* 513M plays in 2022 ([Sporcle blog](https://www.sporcle.com/blog/?p=88268)), and one July 2026 day showed 843,172 plays against a record of 2,225,781 ([stats](https://www.sporcle.com/stats/)) [F].

**Quiz Planet and Trivia Star.** Lotum's Quiz Planet was the top Facebook Instant Game by monthly players in July 2019 and claims 100M+ players ([Lotum](https://lotum.com/en/games)) [F]. Trivia Star offers offline play and a 4.9 rating from about 311K App Store reviews ([App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1508418993)) [S], yet peaked near $4.5K a week on US Android in Q3 2024 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q3-android-top-5-trivia%20games-units-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)) [P]. *Our read:* ratings without a social loop buy little revenue or reach.

**Heads Up! (2013).** Phone-on-forehead charades from Ellen DeGeneres's show. It sold 24M copies at $0.99 and was a top seller from 2013 to 2015 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/06/03/ellen-degeneres-digital-network/)) [P], with extra decks sold in-app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id623592465)). *Our read:* the phone is a prop for a full room, and TV gave it free reach.

### Daily rituals

**Wordle (2021).** Josh Wardle built it for his partner. It grew from 90 players on November 1, 2021 to about 300,000 by late December, and NYT bought it on January 31, 2022 for a low seven-figure sum ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/01/31/1077089945/nyt-wordle)) [P]. *Loop:* one word a day for everyone, six guesses. His prototype allowed unlimited play and he capped it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Wardle)); he said players liked that it wasn't "trying to do anything shady with your data or your eyeballs" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1072980440)) [P]. *Growth:* the spoiler-free grid, shared 46 times per second on average in 2025 ([NBC/TODAY](https://www.nbcchicago.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/hardest-wordle-connections-games-2025/3868912/)). NYT credited Wordle with "tens of millions" of new users and a record quarter for Games subscriber additions ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/new-york-times-earnings-wordle-acquisition-users-0cb11458-5b27-493b-a14b-d0d96eb82f7a.html)) [P]. *Retention:* the streak, which also hurts. CORER ended 5.6M streaks in October 2024 ([TODAY via AOL](https://www.aol.com/exclusive-wordle-puzzle-ended-5-013624721.html)), and the 2022 move to nytimes.com reset some streaks ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/wordle-new-york-times-streaks-broken-nyt-migration-dail-1848525236)) [P]. *Metrics:* 5.3B plays in 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)) and 4.2B solves in 2025 [P].

**NYT Connections and NYT Games.** Connections grew out of an internal game jam, launched in beta in June 2023, and Wyna Liu has edited every board ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Connections)) [P]. *Loop:* sort 16 words into four groups ranked yellow (easiest) to purple (hardest), then share a spoiler-free grid. It is NYT's second most-played game, with 3.3B plays in 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)) and 1.6B solves in 2025; plays and solves are different measures. Across all its games NYT logged 11.2B solves in 2025, with Pips, new that year, at 612M ([NBC/TODAY](https://www.nbcchicago.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/hardest-wordle-connections-games-2025/3868912/)) [P]. A Wrapped-style "Year in Games" recap arrived on December 16, 2025 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/nyt-games-has-a-year-in-review-thing-now-too-203000878.html)). Crossplay, NYT's first two-player game (January 2026), is a free standalone app with friend games and skill-based matchmaking ([AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/consumer-news/2026/01/this-just-in-from-the-new-york-times-its-first-2-player-game-and-an-evolving-business-model/)) that passed 1M downloads within three weeks ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nyt-games-multiplayer-word-game-crossplay-surpasses-one-million-downloads)) [P].

### Duolingo, the gamification benchmark

DAUs reached 58.7M in Q2 2026, up 23% year over year, with 12.7M paid subscribers ([Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)) [F]. Over 10M users hold year-long streaks, and a third of DAUs share a Friend Streak ([Q4 2024 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208825000039/q4fy24duolingo12-31x24shar.htm)) [F]. Ex-CPO Jorge Mazal credits leaderboards and streak features, measured against current-user retention, for 4.5x DAU growth in four years ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)); leagues reportedly lifted learning time 17% [S]. Equipping two streak freezes raised DAU 0.38%, and a streak wager raised day-7 retention 14% ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak), [Econsultancy](https://econsultancy.com/six-a-b-tests-used-by-duolingo-to-tap-into-habit-forming-behaviour/)) [F/S]. In 2025 Energy, which refills on correct-answer runs, began replacing Hearts ([Q2 2025 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208825000165/q2fy25duolingo6-30x25share.htm)) [F], and an "AI-first" memo drew a public backlash ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/duolingo-ceo-admits-controversial-ai-memo-did-not-give-enough-context-insists-company-never-laid-off-full-time-employees)) [P].

### AI-powered quiz products

Generation is now a checkbox. Kahoot builds quizzes from scanned notes or PDFs ([Kahoot help](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/32816978203283)) [F], and Quizizz became Wayground in June 2025 to stress AI-supported lessons ([THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2025/06/24/quizizz-rebrands-as-wayground-announces-new-ai-features.aspx)) [P]. NotebookLM added source-grounded quizzes with an "explain" button on September 8, 2025 ([Google](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/09/flashcards-quizzes-reports-notebook-lm-google-education.html)) [F]. Gizmo wraps AI quizzes in streaks; it grew from about 300,000 users in 2023 to 13M+ and raised $22M in April 2026 ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/04/15/gizmo-secures-22m-series-a-to-advance-ai-powered-education/), [CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/ai-learning-app-gizmo-surpasses-13-million-users-raises-22-million-in-series-a/)) [P]. *Our read:* a quiz about anything is now free and instant. Trust in the questions, and people to play with, are not.

### Patterns that repeat across winners

1. **Seconds to the first question:** no install or account, just a PIN or a link.
2. **A shared object:** the same puzzle for everyone today, or one question in one room, gives people something to talk about.
3. **Sharing that does not spoil:** grids and referral lives make every player a distributor.
4. **Sessions with an end:** one puzzle a day, or seven questions a match. Scarcity prevents burnout.
5. **Skill plus luck:** speed and streak bonuses reward the strong, while wheels and chests keep everyone else in it.
6. **Forgiving habit loops:** streaks, with ways to protect them.
7. **A content engine with a gate:** moderated UGC (Sporcle, Trivia Crack) or one trusted editor (Connections).
8. **Free core, paid edges:** ads, bundles, subscriptions or premium hosting, never a paywall on today's game.

## Implications for our product

1. **Daily Quiz as the front door.** Seven questions, the same for everyone, unlocking at local midnight, no account needed, one run per day. Practice modes live outside it. Budget: first question on screen within 3 seconds.
2. **Spoiler-free share card at launch.** Emoji text plus an Open Graph image (score, per-question squares, time and streak); the link drops friends into the same set.
3. **Async "challenge a friend" duels.** Same seven questions, a 10-second timer, capped speed-weighted points and a doubled final question (QuizUp). No real-time infrastructure needed.
4. **Host mode for parties and classrooms.** Big-screen host view, with phones joining by six-digit code or QR. Host tools: a friendly-name generator on by default, kick, a player cap and join rate limits. Kahoot-style scoring (up to 1,000, speed plus streak) with an Accuracy toggle. Scoring and timing are server-authoritative.
5. **Optional comeback mode.** A "Chaos" variant with chest rewards (steal, swap, multiplier, nothing) beside a pure-skill "Classic." Hosts can turn sabotage off, as in Gimkit.
6. **Forgiving streaks that we never break.** Earned freezes (equip up to two, as Duolingo tested) plus a 24-hour repair window. Store streaks server-side for accounts (local fallback for guests) and test that they survive every migration.
7. **Leagues and friend streaks in phase two,** opened only once weekly actives can fill them. An empty leaderboard is worse than none.
8. **A type-in "deep cut" round** with rarity scoring (Immaculate Grid, Krillion) and as-you-type matching (Sporcle). Seed rarity from editor estimates until traffic arrives.
9. **Question quality is the product.** Each question stores a source URL, category, difficulty and last-verified date. User submissions pass community flags, then an editor; expect to publish a small share. Localize by region later.
10. **AI drafts, humans publish.** Creators can paste text or a URL to draft a quiz but must edit before publishing, and public quizzes carry an "AI-assisted" label. The daily stays human-written and fact-checked.
11. **Monetize the edges, later.** No ads in the daily at launch. Premium can follow: past-daily archive, unlimited practice, large rooms and team branding. Never sell lives that block play.
12. **Live events as seasoning:** a weekly 10-minute "Showdown" with no cash prizes and an async fallback if realtime fails.
13. **Use the web's strengths:** an installable PWA, fast first load, indexable pages per public quiz and category, and a December recap that restarts sharing.
14. **Post-game feedback:** a speed percentile ("faster than 72% of players," as Clues by Sam does) and a one-line explanation with a source for each miss.
15. **Instrument from day one:** day-1 and day-7 return, shares per daily, invite acceptance and time to first question.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Cash prizes as growth.** HQ's prize bill outran its income, and payout complaints followed.
- **A fragile live layer.** HQ's lag broke trust.
- **A social network instead of a game.** QuizUp's community cost more than it earned.
- **Novelty without iteration.** HQ "ceased to evolve."
- **Lives that gate play.** Trivia Crack drew criticism for it, and Duolingo dropped Hearts.
- **Open codes without moderation.** Kahoot PINs attract bots and offensive names.
- **Trusting the client.** Blooket and Gimkit cheat scripts thrive on browser-side state.
- **Unvetted user content.** A racist user-submitted question went viral although Trivia Crack never approved it ([Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/trivia-crack/)).
- **Breaking streaks.** Wordle's migration reset them, and players were furious.
- **"AI-first" messaging.** Duolingo's users heard cost cutting at their expense.
- **Single points of failure outside the product.** QuizUp leaned on a TV deal, HQ on a star host.
- **Paywalling the daily.** Wordle fans feared it at the sale; NYT kept Wordle free.

## Open questions

1. Which wedge leads: the solo daily, async duels, hosted rooms or user-made quizzes? The evidence favors the daily plus duels, with hosting close behind.
2. Do we serve under-13 classrooms (COPPA and school-privacy duties), or launch 13+? Legal review needed.
3. How many concurrent players can one host room hold on the Supabase free plan? Architecture should load-test it.
4. What player count makes rarity scores and leagues meaningful, and how do we bridge the cold start?
5. What retention is realistic for a new web app? Public benchmarks come from giant installed bases.
6. Will AI-drafted questions pass fact-checking at an acceptable rate and cost?
7. How do we define "play" and "complete"? Sources mix plays with solves.
8. Which categories and regions come first? Regional sets mattered for Trivia Crack, but we have not sized demand.
