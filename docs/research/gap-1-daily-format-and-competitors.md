# Gap 1: The Daily's format and its direct competitors

*Follow-up track, compiled 31 October 2026.* **Method note.** The workflow's shared WebSearch budget (200 calls per turn, all agents) was spent before this track began, and direct fetches failed because the hosts tried (whats-on-netflix.com, en.wikipedia.org) are outside this session's network allowlist. The evidence is search results earlier tracks gathered today, re-read from their transcripts, plus this folder's eight reports. No Similarweb, Semrush, subreddit or Google Trends data could be collected; Open questions ends with a verification queue. Tags: **[F]** company-reported, **[P]** press, **[S]** secondary or single low-confidence source, **[K]** background knowledge not re-checked here. *Our read* marks interpretation.

## Summary

- **The daily trivia slot is taken.** In October 2026 it holds Krillion (typed answers scored by rarity, July 2026), Netflix Star: Daily Trivia (on TV since August 2026), Immaculate Grid (sports, owned by Sports Reference) and Bracket City (nested clues, licensed to The Atlantic), plus a long tail on directory sites.
- **No ten-question multiple-choice daily with a large organic audience turned up.** The multiple-choice dailies found (Quizl, Britannica's Tightrope) appear only as directory listings without player numbers. Big multiple-choice audiences come from a platform's distribution (Netflix, newspapers) or from cash (HQ). With search down, read this as a strong hint, not proof.
- **The breakouts ask players to recall or estimate, and every attempt returns information.** A miss on Framed shows another still, Worldle points toward the country, Immaculate Grid and Krillion pay more for rare answers, and TimeGuessr scores how far off you were. Shares show that path or a brag number, never the answer.
- **Few numbers are public:** about one Wordle share per three solves in 2025 (our arithmetic), 84% next-day return at LinkedIn, 50,000+ daily players for Clues by Sam and tens of thousands for Immaculate Grid. No independent web daily has published retention.
- **First players came from shared results in group chats and feeds;** directories, press roundups, maker sites and answer pages followed. Wordle went from 90 players to about 300,000 in two months, and Immaculate Grid sold to Sports Reference after three.
- **Recommendation: three mysteries a day, four clues each, plus the closest-number encore.** Each mystery is one answer, clued from hardest to easiest. Players type a guess with suggestions, and clue 4 adds four options so nobody is shut out. Points run 4, 3, 2 and 1, out of 12.
- **The share** becomes a spoiler-free grid: one four-square row per mystery, the score out of 12, the percentile once 50 players have finished, and the encore's error.
- **Content:** 1,460 sealed items a year instead of 3,650, and 240 for 60 launch Dailies instead of 600.
- **Past Dailies** become free, indexable explainer pages once they close everywhere; replays are free for 7 days and a Plus perk beyond. **The first three channels** are group chats (through share and challenge links), daily-game directories, then one post each on Reddit and Hacker News.

### 3. Is there a large ten-question multiple-choice daily?

We found none. Earlier searches for new trivia dailies returned Krillion first and multiple-choice games only as listings, none with download or player numbers ([playdaily.org](https://playdaily.org/resources/stories/best-daily-trivia-games-like-wordle), [dailydles](https://dailydles.com/categories/trivia)). Ten-question sets survive as editorial, in weekly news quizzes and some UK papers' daily quizzes [K], with no public figures.

Revealed preference agrees. When adding knowledge games, The Atlantic licensed Bracket City and Sports Reference bought Immaculate Grid. LinkedIn built Pinpoint and Crossclimb, the latter feeding trivia clues into a word ladder [K]. Netflix may be the exception, but its format is unconfirmed. Lotum's Quiz Planet claims 100M+ players for multiple-choice trivia, built on Facebook's distribution ([Lotum](https://lotum.com/en/games)) [F].

We could not collect player or creator complaints, so these reasons are our read:
- **Lookups.** Scott Rogowsky built TextSavvy on word puzzles because "in the age of AI, trivia is unfortunately killed" ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/hq-trivia-catapulted-host-scott-191520139.html), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/20/remember-hq-quiz-daddy-scott-rogowsky-is-back-with-textsavvy-a-daily-mobile-game-show/)). Yet Immaculate Grid thrives under the stats company whose sites hold every answer, so lookups seem to matter less when nothing is at stake.
- **Nothing to talk about.** "7/10" is a score, not a story.
- **No way to get closer.** With four options you know it or you guess.
- **A low ceiling.** Experts tie at 10/10, so time decides, which rewards speed and lookups.
- **Cost.** Ten checked items a day is the heaviest content load of any format here.

### 4. How the breakouts found their first players

- **Wordle.** Built for Josh Wardle's partner and first shared in a family group chat [K]: 90 players on 1 November 2021, about 300,000 by early January ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/01/31/1077089945/nyt-wordle)). The share button came in mid-December, copying emoji grids that players in New Zealand typed by hand [K]. Wardle at GDC: "If you can only play once a day and if it was the same word for everyone, there was suddenly this social aspect to the game" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success), [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/78481/gdc-josh-wardle-shares-his-incorrect-tips-for-wordles-success/)).
- **Immaculate Grid.** Spread among baseball fans, and AOL called the new sports quizzes "viral" ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/enjoy-wordle-sports-trivia-quizzes-080344683.html)) [S]. Sports Reference bought it about three months in and carried it across its stats sites and other sports ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/immaculate-grid-tv-game-show-trivia-sports-reference-baseball-brady/), [Baseball-Reference](https://www.baseball-reference.com/bullpen/Immaculate_Grid)); copies such as Movie Grid followed ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/11/01/arts/movie-grid-trivia-game-wordle/)).
- **Krillion.** Listed on maker sites ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/krillion), a Product Hunt tracker, and [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/chaodit/project/krillion--play-and-explore-rareanswer)); within about three months it led a directory and drew an answers page and clones.
- **Bracket City.** Had a loyal audience before tripling at The Atlantic; a builder for your own Bracket City puzzles reached Hacker News ([Show HN](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/45772650)).
- **Clues by Sam.** Solo, covered by games press ([Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/clues-by-sam-wordle-daily-puzzle-game/)) and directories ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/games/clues-by-sam/)); about eight months to 50,000.
- **Puzzmo.** A waitlist drew 100,000 people within about a month ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/zach-gage-and-orta-theroxs-puzzle-platform-puzzmo-acquired-by-hearst-newspapers)), riding the 15M installs of Zach Gage's earlier games ([INMA](https://www.inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hearst-cracks-the-engagement-code-with-puzzmo)).

Channels, strongest evidence first:
1. **Shared results in group chats and feeds:** every breakout, with volume data from Wordle.
2. **Directories and "-dle" lists** ([All the Dles](https://allthedles.com/), [Listdle](https://listdle.com/), [Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/games/bracket-city/), [playdaily.org](https://playdaily.org/resources/stories/best-daily-trivia-games-like-wordle)). They rank by popularity, so they amplify traction more than create it (our read).
3. **Communities matching the subject:** baseball fans, puzzle and developer forums.
4. **A publisher or platform** (The Atlantic, Sports Reference, Hearst, Netflix, LinkedIn), not open to us at launch.
5. **Today's-answer pages** (The Word Finder, Sportsdunia): a lagging sign of search demand.
6. **Streams:** one guide says the size game Size It Up went around streams in October 2026 ([playdaily.org](https://playdaily.org/resources/stories/best-daily-trivia-games-like-wordle)) [S].

No game disclosed when it passed 10,000 daily players. Breakouts showed themselves within one to three months; Clues by Sam's slower climb is the exception.

### 5. Disclosed share and retention numbers

- **Wordle:** 46 shares a second over 4.2B solves in 2025 is about 4.0M shares a day for 11.5M solves, roughly one in three.
- **LinkedIn:** 84% next day and 80% a week later, among people who already open LinkedIn daily: a ceiling, not a benchmark for a standalone site (our read).
- **Clues by Sam and Immaculate Grid:** player counts, no retention.
- **Nothing** from Krillion, Bracket City, Framed, TimeGuessr or Netflix.

*Our read:* the synthesis's 20% share target suits a grid that tells a story far better than "8/10 in 1:42".

### 6. The pattern

Four traits recur: one small shared object a day; recall or estimation, with each attempt narrowing the answer; a low floor with a high ceiling; and a share showing a path or a brag number. Ten multiple-choice questions meet the first loosely and miss the rest.

## Implications for our product

### The Daily: three mysteries, four clues each

- **Shape.** Three mysteries from three categories, easiest first (peak-end, 03), in about 2 to 4 minutes (est.). Categories rotate so each appears every three or four days; `global` items only.
- **Clues.** One answer, four clues, hardest first, like quiz bowl and the room clue ladder in 6.2 (03). Calibrate with test-solvers so a typical player scores 6 or 7 of 12, inside the 55 to 70% band for ranked play (03).
- **Answering.** Type a guess and pick from suggestions: a closed list for the answer type (countries, people, films, elements), drawn from Wikidata (CC0, 7.12), in an accessible combobox. A wrong guess or "Next clue" reveals the next clue. Clue 4 adds four options, so anyone who can't recall or type the name still finishes.
- **Scoring.** 4, 3, 2 or 1 points by the clue that solved it, 0 if unsolved: out of 12. Ties break on the encore's error, then time, with the hideable stopwatch and no countdown. Implausibly fast clue-1 sweeps stay out of the percentile (section 9, row 24).
- **Reveal.** The answer, all four clues, a "why" of 35 words or fewer, and sources. After the player commits: "14% got this on clue 1." A most-common-wrong-guess line is safe, since guesses are listed entities, never free text.
- **Multiple choice stays everywhere else.** Practice, Challenges, Host mode and the quiz editor keep four options.

Why this format:
- It joins two proven mechanics: a new clue after each miss (Framed, Pinpoint [K]) and a suggestion box over a closed list (Worldle, Immaculate Grid).
- It needs no crowd to score fairly on day one, unlike rarity.
- It protects trust: one subject's sources cover all four clues, and closed lists keep right answers from being marked wrong.
- The clues are the explanations, which is our edge.
- Early solves separate the experts who would all score 10/10 on multiple choice, so the top of the percentile means something.

Why not the alternatives:
- **Krillion-style rarity.** It needs a crowd or editor guesses, and one missing valid answer marks a correct player wrong. Krillion stops lookups with a 25-second clock, which our no-countdown rule (WCAG 2.2.1) excludes, and it already leads with the idea. Keep rarity for a weekly "Deep Cut" once 500 players finish each Daily (our threshold; 6.1 Could).
- **One mystery a day.** Cheapest (730 items a year with the encore), but one unfamiliar category decides the day and only five scores exist. It is the fallback if content operations stall.
- **Estimation only.** Numbers are easy to check but easier to look up, and without TimeGuessr's photos they get dry. It stays as the encore.
- **Ten multiple-choice questions.** It has the weakest share and the heaviest load. No breakout uses it.

**Test before locking.** Greybox the ladder beside the ten-question set and run persona playtests on completion and "would you share this?". In beta, give a random slice of new players the ten-question set behind a flag; keep the ladder if its share rate is at least 1.5 times higher with D1 no lower. If typing causes drop-off, move the options to clue 3.

### The share artifact

Text first, link last, no answers. G is a green square, R a red one and a dot a black one; category names render as their emoji:

```
[Name] #42  9/12  top 29% today
Geography   G . . .
Film & TV   R R G .
Science     R R R G
Encore      off by 3%
[name].app/d/42
```

- The percentile line appears once 50 players have finished (8.8).
- The link opens that Daily if it is still open for the reader, otherwise today's.
- The preview image shows the grid and score, never answers.
- It replaces the 6.4 template, "Daily #12: 8/10 in 1:42. Streak 6."

- Replace the 300-item sealed reserve with 30 spare days (90 mysteries, 30 encores).
- The weekly need falls from 70 sealed items to 28. The risk remains, so keep the 14-day buffer alert.

### Past Dailies: free explainers, paid replays

- **A free explainer page per Daily** (for example `/daily/42`), published once it has closed in every time zone, about 50 hours after it first unlocks at local midnight. It shows each answer with its clues and sourced why-line, plus the encore and how everyone did, and one button into today's Daily. Answer pages follow every breakout anyway; ours would be original and one tap from play, much as NYT runs its own Wordle answer column [K]. Never publish answers while a Daily is open anywhere.
- **Replays.** The last 7 Dailies stay free forever for streak repair (decision 12); older replays with scoring are Plus. Krillion sells its archive while The Word Finder lists every answer, so public answers do not seem to kill archive revenue (our read).

### The first three launch channels

1. **Group chats, via share and challenge links.** Seed about 100 friends and family (Wordle started from 90) and watch share rate and invite acceptance before a wider push.
2. **Daily-game directories.** On launch day, submit to All the Dles, dailydles, Listdle, playdaily.org and Thinky Games with screenshots and a sample share, then pitch "games like Wordle" roundup writers.
3. **One post each on Reddit and Hacker News** (r/WebGames and r/trivia, plus a Show HN) with the build story and the fact-checking angle, and same-day Product Hunt and Peerlist listings.

Later: a stream-friendly mode and publisher licensing (Bracket City's path). Answer search waits for demand.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **A share that says only "8/10":** no path, so the group chat has nothing to react to.
- **Cloning Krillion:** months late, and it needs the clock we ruled out.
- **Free-text guesses shown to anyone:** crowd stats name listed entities only (constraint 9).
- **Answer lists that reject right answers:** accept aliases and both spellings (7.9), and let reports void a mystery (7.11).
- **Publishing answers while a Daily is open.**
- **Leaning on someone else's surface:** Netflix pulled its earlier trivia titles in 2024, and Spotify closed Heardle within a year of buying it [K].
- **Charging for the Daily, or taking back free archive days.**
- **Chasing answer-search traffic before anyone searches for us.**

## Open questions

- **Netflix Star:** questions per day, format, scoring and share.
- **Krillion:** share text, player numbers and archive price.
- **Thrice,** reportedly a three-clue daily from pub-quiz company Geeks Who Drink [K], may be the closest precedent. How big is it?
- **Typing on phones:** does a suggestion box cut completion compared with taps? Playtests first, then the beta flag.
- **Small answer lists** (planets, chess pieces) may give a mystery away through the suggestion box; use them only for easy mysteries.
