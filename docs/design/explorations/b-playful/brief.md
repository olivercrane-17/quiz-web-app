# Exploration B: bold playful

*Design exploration, 31 October 2026. Direction: the energy of the best casual games and language-learning apps. Proposed name: **Zooks**.*

Open [`index.html`](./index.html) in a browser to see every screen in light and dark side by side, with buttons that replay the clue reveal and the answer reveal. The PNGs in this folder are the same screens, captured by [`capture.js`](./capture.js) at 390 by 844 CSS px (and the share image at 1200 by 630).

**The pitch in five lines**

1. Zooks is the noise you make when clue 2 lands and you suddenly know, so the game is named after its best moment.
2. Bold playful means saturated toy-box color on warm paper, ink outlines with a solid ledge underneath, and buttons that press, sink, drop and pop on real springs.
3. Shape tells difficulty before color does: pebble, cookie, burst and target mark the three mysteries and the Encore on every screen and in the share.
4. The two o's are eyes, the only face we use, so the brand has character without a mascot to animate or explain.
5. Joy never costs access: every verdict carries an icon and a word, and all 80 measured color pairs pass WCAG in both themes.

## 1. Name

### Recommended: Zooks

Say it like "books". It is the clipped form of "gadzooks", an English exclamation of surprise dated to 1625 to 1635 ([Dictionary.com](https://dictionary.com/browse/zooks)). Green's Dictionary of Slang lists "zooks!" on its own as a general exclamation, used jokingly in the 20th century ([Green's](https://greensdictofslang.com/entry/gqjx2dq)).

Why it works:

- **It is the sound the product is built to produce.** The best moment in a clue ladder is the half second when a clue lands and you know. That moment needs a noise, and this is it.
- **The name doubles as the celebration.** A clue-1 solve says "Zooks!" and so does an Encore bullseye. A name you can shout is rare, and it gives the voice a signature word that costs nothing.
- **The two o's are eyes.** That pair of eyes is the whole character: no mascot, no body, no mouth, no name. In the wordmark they glance up and to the right, toward the content.
- **It travels.** Five letters in a group chat ("Zooks #42 · 9/12") and one syllable on a call. There is no local idiom to explain in Manchester, Mumbai, Toronto or Melbourne.
- **It says nothing about quizzes or trivia**, so it is suggestive rather than descriptive, which is the better position when filing a trademark.

What I checked: web searches on 31 October 2026 found no game or app called Zooks. The nearest results were OOKS, a preschool brand moving into apps ([Licensing Source](https://licensingsource.net/ooks-heads-into-apps-and-animated-shorts)), and zoo games such as Zookemon ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1593869420)). A web search is not a trademark search. The launch report should carry a proper clearance (software and entertainment classes) and a domain check; `zooks.app` in the prototype is a placeholder.

Risks: "zoo" sits inside the word, so some people will think of animals first (the eyes help, because they turn it into a face). And "gadzooks" began in the 1600s as a minced oath, usually traced to "God's hooks" ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/gadzooks)). Nobody hears it that way today, but the owner should know before picking it.

### Alternate 1: Kaboo

Peekaboo meets kaboom: the answer hides behind the clues, then it lands. The eyes sit at the end of the word, peeking out. Searches found nothing called Kaboo. Nearby names are Kabu, a 2018 Build-A-Bear lifestyle brand that shipped its own game app ([Franchising.com](https://southafrica.franchising.com/news/20180731_welcome_to_the_world_of_kabutrade_a_new_lifestyle_.html)), and Kuboo, a kids' virtual world ([Owler](https://www.owler.com/reports/kuboo/kuboo-posted-a-video-%22kuboo--see-all-there-is-to-d/1449843591945)). It skews younger than Zooks. Pick it if the owner wants the warmest possible brand.

### Alternate 2: Dekko

British slang for a quick look ("have a dekko"), borrowed from Hindi *dekho*, "look!", and recorded in English from the 1890s ([Dictionary.com](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/dekko), [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/dekkoed), [World Wide Words](https://www.worldwidewords.org/qa-dek1.html)). Each clue is literally a dekko, and the word already belongs to the English of two of the four cities the synthesis names. The wordmark would use one large eye for the final o. Checks: Dekko was an AR start-up that closed in 2014 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/09/07/dekko)), and Dekkoo, an active film-streaming service, is one letter away ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dekkoo)). The word reached English through the British Army in India, a history some players will notice.

## 2. Personality and voice

Zooks is the friend at the pub-quiz table who knows one weird fact about everything and is delighted when you know one too. Four traits:

- **Curious.** Shares a fact the way you'd share gossip, and always says why.
- **Cheeky, never smug.** Teases the question, never the player.
- **Generous.** Credits close calls that were close, and explains every answer.
- **Quick.** Short lines, then it gets out of the way.

Rules:

- "Zooks!" is reserved for real moments: a clue-1 solve, an Encore bullseye, a personal best, a perfect Daily. Overused, it stops meaning anything.
- At most one exclamation mark per screen. Praise the play, not the person ("Clue 1. Lovely." rather than "You're a genius!").
- No guilt and no fake urgency. "So close!" only when the guess sat inside the bullseye or close tolerance (decision 2).
- Plain English that works for every region we serve: no league-specific sports metaphors, no US-only idioms. Sentence case everywhere, numerals for numbers, no all caps and no italics.

### The share text

Decision 2 leaves the glyphs to design. They have to read well in WhatsApp and iMessage and when a screen reader speaks them.

```
Zooks #42 · 9/12 · Encore 4% off 🎯
Landmark, clue 1: ✅⬜⬜⬜
City, clue 3: 🟧🟧✅⬜
Person, clue 2: 🟧✅⬜⬜
Beat 72% so far today
zooks.app/d/42
```

- Words lead every row, because screen readers read emoji one by one ([report 05](../../../research/05-ux-ui-best-practices.md)). A listener hears "City, clue 3" first and can skip the squares.
- The legend: ✅ the clue that solved it, 🟧 a clue you used, ⬜ a clue you never needed, ❌ the end of a missed mystery. ✅ is a shape as well as a color, so the solve survives color blindness and grayscale.
- Rows name the answer type (shown before anyone guesses), never the answer. 🎯 appears only inside the bullseye tolerance, and the percentile line only after 50 finishers (synthesis 8.8). The link stays path-only.
- ⬜ rather than ⬛ because a black square vanishes in dark-mode chats.

## 3. Color system

### The idea

A toy box on warm paper. Saturated fills that look like you could pick them up, each held by an ink outline with a solid ledge underneath. The four rounds of every Daily get four colors: **sun** (Mystery 1), **gum** (Mystery 2), **grape** (Mystery 3, also the brand) and **sky** (Encore). **Leaf** and **tomato** carry verdicts, **flame** and **ice** carry streaks and freezes.

Semantic names sit on top (`--bg`, `--text`, `--primary`, `--correct`, `--incorrect`, `--m1` to `--m4`, `--focus`), so a theme or the high-contrast setting swaps values without touching components. Dark mode follows the system and can be overridden (`data-theme`); the same token blocks drive both.

### Rules

- **Never color alone.** Every state is a fill plus an icon plus a word, and usually motion too ([WCAG 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)).
- **The outline is the accessibility feature.** In light theme every state fill sits inside a 2 px ink outline, so the color touching a fill is always ink (6.06:1 or better) and ink against the page is 15.8:1. A green fill straight on white would manage only 1.99:1; the outline means that never happens. In dark theme the fills sit inside near-black outlines and stand 5.57:1 or more off the card.
- **Dark mode is not inverted light mode.** Surfaces sit near #121212 with a violet cast and accents are slightly desaturated, never pure black with neon ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)). Neutral components swap ink outlines for chalk outlines (`#6C6592`, at least 3.11:1 against every surface), and the ledge takes the same chalk color so the 3D still reads.
- **No translucent panels behind text** (synthesis 8.9).
- **Forced colors:** borders and icons carry every state; the correct answer gets a 4 px `Highlight` border and the wrong pick a 4 px dashed `CanvasText` border.

### Measured contrast

Every number below comes from [`contrast.js`](./contrast.js), which reads the token values out of `styles.css`, so the table cannot drift from the code. Text needs 4.5:1 ([WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)); outlines and states need 3:1 ([WCAG 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). The protan and deutan columns repeat the measurement after simulating protanopia and deuteranopia at full severity ([Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2009.113)). Run `node contrast.js` to regenerate.

### Color vision

About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have a color vision deficiency, mostly red-green ([Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/)), the very pair a quiz leans on. Luminance contrast between the correct and incorrect fills is low by design (1.41:1 light, 1.34:1 dark), so contrast.js also measures perceptual distance in OKLab ([Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)), where under 0.05 is hard to tell apart:

| Pair | Normal | Protan | Deutan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leaf vs tomato, light | 0.323 | 0.187 | **0.054** |
| Leaf vs tomato, dark | 0.291 | 0.161 | **0.046** |
| Blue vs orange (high-contrast setting), light | 0.331 | 0.274 | 0.329 |
| Blue vs orange, dark | 0.295 | 0.242 | 0.287 |

For a deuteranope our green and tomato fills nearly merge. That is why the icon and the word always travel with the color, why the wrong pick also shakes, and why the high-contrast feedback setting (`data-feedback="contrast"`, blue and orange as in [Wordle](https://www.nytimes.com/games/wordle/index.html)) should sit in the first settings sheet a player opens rather than three levels deep. The share uses ✅ for the same reason.

- Sans-serif type helps readers with dyslexia and italics hurt ([Rello and Baeza-Yates](https://doi.org/10.1145/2513383.2513447)); the BDA adds bold over italics, no all caps, 1.5 line spacing and off-white backgrounds ([BDA](https://www.bdadyslexia.org.uk/advice/employers/creating-a-dyslexia-friendly-workplace/dyslexia-friendly-style-guide)). ## 5. Spacing, radius and elevation

**Spacing** sits on a 4 px grid: `--s1` 4, `--s2` 8, `--s3` 12, `--s4` 16, `--s5` 20, `--s6` 24, `--s8` 32, `--s10` 40, `--s12` 48. Screen gutters are 16 px (12 px under 360 px wide), answers stack with 12 px gaps, and cards stack with 14 to 16 px.

**Radius:** chips 999 px, small 10, key badges 12, buttons and answers 18, cards 24, sheets 28, the Daily hero 32. Corners grow with the size of the object, so nothing looks pinched.

**Elevation** is a solid ledge, not a blur. It reads like a sticker or a keycap, and it stays crisp on cheap screens.

| Level | Recipe | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Flat | No ledge | Dimmed answers, used states, wells |
| e1 | 2 px outline, 3 px ledge | Chips, small buttons |
| e2 | 2 px outline, 4 px ledge | Buttons, answers, cards, clues; the floating tab bar adds the soft shadow below |
| e3 | 2 px outline, 6 px ledge, plus a soft `0 14px 28px` shadow at 14% | The Daily hero and the suggestion popover |
| Sheet | 2 px top outline, 28 px top corners, soft upward shadow | Explanation sheet |

Pressing moves a control down 2 px while its ledge shrinks from 4 px to 2 px (60 ms), and the answer commits on release, so sliding off cancels ([WCAG 2.5.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html)). After a reveal, unchosen answers lose their ledge and drop 4 px, so their bottom edge stays exactly where it was: they sink into the page instead of fading out. In dark theme, neutral components use the chalk outline for both outline and ledge, and colored ones use a deep outline or a darker shade of their own hue (`#3A2A9E` under grape).

Shapes go from smooth to spiky as the rounds get harder, so the outline alone says how hard a round is; the color and the word (Easy, Medium, Hard, Encore) say it twice more. The geometry is generated, not drawn by hand, so the set stays consistent as it grows (the family borrows from Material 3 Expressive's shape library, [Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Each shape has a 5-unit ink stroke and lifts on a 4 px drop-shadow ledge. Upcoming rounds show the same shape in a muted outline and done rounds carry a check. The shapes come back on the results rows and in the share image, and they double as personal-best confetti.

### The eyes

The eyes are the only face in the identity. They live in the wordmark and, later, in two system moments: the "Checking..." wait (they glance left and right) and the offline state (closed). They never go on shapes or buttons, and they never get a mouth. In idle they blink every five or six seconds (the gallery shows it); under reduced motion they hold still.

### Icons

Drawn for this exploration as an inline SVG sprite on a 24 px grid, with 2.5 px strokes and round caps and joins so they match the outlines. Smaller sizes thicken the stroke (18 px at 2.75, 14 px at 3.25) so weight looks the same at every size. Only the flame and the sparkle are filled. The set covers close, check, cross, flame, snowflake, settings, stats, today, practice, groups, share, stopwatch, sound off, report flag, external link, arrow, search, map pin, next clue, sparkle and clock, plus keyboard glyphs. Every icon that carries meaning has a text label beside it or an accessible name.

## 7. Motion

Principles:

1. **Physical.** Things press, sink, drop and pop as if they had weight. Motion uses damped springs sampled into CSS `linear()` easings ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/easing-function/linear)), so a bounce costs no JavaScript and no animation library in the first bundle.
2. **Short and skippable.** UI motion runs 100 to 500 ms ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). The longest automatic sequence, the answer reveal, settles by about 1.6 s, and a tap jumps to the end.
3. **Feedback scales with success.** A pop and a chime for a correct answer, a "Zooks!" sticker for a clue-1 solve, a sprinkle of mini shapes for a personal best, and the full burst only for a perfect Daily. Report 03 and a pre-registered study warn that overloaded feedback can lower felt competence ([KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000171104/152952099)).
4. **Nothing moves the layout.** New clues fill reserved slots, sheets rise over empty space, dimmed answers sink in place and buttons never move between questions (CLS budget 0.05). The sheet carries a 48 px skirt below the screen so its spring overshoot never opens a gap.
5. **Reduced motion is a first-class mode.** The OS setting or the in-app switch turns every transform into a 150 ms fade, removes the shake and the confetti, shows final numbers without counting and stops the eyes blinking ([WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Nothing ever flashes more than three times a second ([WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)).

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--dur-press` | 60 ms | Press state |
| `--dur-lock` | 150 ms | Lock-in |
| `--dur-fade` | 150 ms | Every reduced-motion substitute |
| `--dur-shake` | 300 ms | Wrong pick: 6 px, two cycles |
| `--dur-sheet` | 380 ms | Explanation sheet |
| `--dur-clue` | 450 ms | New clue drop |
| `--dur-reveal` | 470 ms | Correct answer pop |
| `--dur-count` | 500 ms | Count-ups |
| `--ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)` | Color, ledge and settle changes |
| `--ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` | Shake |
| `--spring-pop` | Damping ratio 0.5: settles in 472 ms, 16% overshoot | Pops, chips, tags |
| `--spring-drop` | Damping ratio 0.58: 446 ms, 11% overshoot | Clue drop, number tick |
| `--spring-soft` | Damping ratio 0.72: 375 ms, 4% overshoot | Sheets, option rows |

### The answer reveal (practice and every multiple-choice question)

This follows the synthesis timeline (8.3); the prototype's "Replay the answer reveal" button plays it.

| Time | What happens | Motion and sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0 ms | Press | Down 2 px, ledge 4 to 2 px, 60 ms ease-out; soft wooden tap |
| 150 ms | Lock-in | The pick fills grape and holds; 10 ms vibration on Android if switched on |
| 400 ms | Reveal | The right answer turns leaf and pops (scale 0.94 to 1, spring-pop); a wrong pick turns tomato and shakes 6 px twice; the others sink flat (200 ms ease-out) |
| 520 ms | Labels | "Correct answer" and "Your answer" pop in (spring-pop) |
| 400 to 900 ms | Count | Run score counts up in tabular figures; rising two-note chime, or a soft felt thud |
| 1,200 ms | Explanation | Sheet rises (spring-soft, 380 ms); a polite status region says "Not quite. You chose Mercury. The answer is Jupiter." |
| Until tapped | Read | Continue waits where the answers were. A 35-word explanation takes about 9 s to read ([Brysbaert](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2019.104047)), so nothing advances on its own |

### The clue reveal (Daily mystery, after a wrong guess or Next clue)

"Replay the clue reveal" plays this one.

| Time | What happens | Motion and sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0 ms | Guess commits | The suggestion list folds away (120 ms); a "Not Lima" chip pops onto the clue it answered (spring-pop, 300 ms); soft thud |
| 250 ms | Old clue settles | Background to well, ledge 4 to 2 px, text to ink-2 (200 ms ease-out) |
| 300 ms | New clue | Drops 14 px into its reserved slot and grows from 0.96 to 1 (spring-drop, 450 ms); a paper flick plus one marimba note, lower than the last |
| 420 ms | "New" tag | Pops (spring-pop) |
| 480 ms | Worth ticks down | The worth chip rides on the new clue, so once it lands its number slides in from above (4 becomes 3, spring-drop, 240 ms) and one pip empties |
| 500 ms | Announce | A polite live region reads the new clue; focus stays in the field so typing carries on |
| Clue 4 | Options | Four options rise one after another (40 ms stagger, spring-soft) |

## 8. Sound and haptics

**Concept: a wooden toy box.** Marimba, woodblock, felt and paper, the sounds of things you'd find in a toy chest. Everything is synthesized in Web Audio from oscillators and a little filtered noise, which keeps the whole palette to a few KB of code against the 30 KB budget, and the `AudioContext` defaults to "ambient", so it mixes with the player's own music instead of pausing it ([Audio Session](https://w3c.github.io/audio-session/)). The context starts on the first tap ([Web Audio](https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/)). Effects start off on phones behind a visible speaker toggle that remembers the choice, the host screen starts with sound on, music never autoplays, and nothing is ever said by sound alone (synthesis 8.7).

| Event | Sound | Notes and length | Peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Press | Woodblock tock | Noise through a 1.8 kHz band-pass, 40 ms | -26 dBFS |
| Correct | Marimba, rising fourth | G5 (784 Hz) then C6 (1,047 Hz), about 280 ms | -18 dBFS |
| Clue-1 solve | Marimba arpeggio | C6, E6, G6, 60 ms apart, about 320 ms, under the "Zooks!" sticker | -18 dBFS |
| Wrong | Felt bonk | Sine sliding from 220 Hz to 165 Hz, low-passed at 900 Hz, 160 ms; never a buzzer | -22 dBFS |
| New clue | Paper flick plus one note | 25 ms of high-passed noise, then A5, E5 and C5 for clues 2, 3 and 4: you can hear the points draining | -22 dBFS |
| Suggestion picked | Tick | 2.4 kHz, 15 ms | -28 dBFS |
| Encore bullseye | Rising whoop and a pop | Sine sweep from 440 Hz to 880 Hz in 180 ms, then 20 ms of noise | -18 dBFS |
| Encore close | Two soft notes | E5 then G5 | -22 dBFS |
| Personal best | Four-note jingle | C5, E5, G5, C6 in 480 ms | -18 dBFS |
| Perfect Daily | The jingle plus a shimmer | 400 ms filtered-noise tail, once, skippable | -18 dBFS |

Haptics are Android extras behind a toggle (tap 10 ms, correct 20 ms, wrong 30-40-30 ms), because Safari has no vibration API ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate)). Nothing depends on them.

## 11. Open questions

1. **Name clearance.** Zooks needs a trademark search and a domain check before anyone grows attached to it.
2. **Display font weight.** Fredoka with both axes is 68 KB for Latin. Subset it, or drop the width axis outside the wordmark, before the performance budget is tested.
3. **Short phones.** The keyboard-up Mystery layout fits four suggestions at 844 px tall. On a 667 px phone roughly two fit, so used clues should collapse to one line while the keyboard is up. That needs a playtest.
4. **Default feedback colors.** Green and tomato read as joyful and familiar, and blue and orange hold up for deuteranopes. Keep green and tomato as the default with blue and orange one tap away, or test making blue and orange the default?
5. **A tab bar on the web.** It costs 92 px on Home. Groups and Stats could live in the header instead; worth an A/B once Groups exist.
6. **Recorded or synthesized sound.** Synthesis keeps the palette tiny; recorded marimba would sound richer at the cost of bytes and an audio-sprite pipeline.
